A little late to the party with this one because I took my sweet time (but not enough) playing this game, with a final completion time of 95 hours, but better late than never.
I think Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave is an easy contender for game of the year thus far. Granted, I haven’t yet played Splatoon Raiders, Ocarina of Time (remake) or Grand Theft Auto VI, but I have played a lot of the other games widely considered for the part, and this game clears. Every game is, of course, different, but nothing hits like a Fire Emblem game that is actually *good* (looking at you Engage). Fortune’s Weave isn’t without its flaws, but it is still one of the most enjoyable games I’ve played in years, and one of the **very** few that can keep me fully enamoured for it’s frankly very long runtime AND leave me wanting more at the end. Even Persona 5 Royal, which at time of writing is fighting for the spot of my favourite game of all time, didn’t quite manage to keep me 100% hooked in and enthralled. And as someone who has Fire Emblem: Three Houses in their top 5 games of all time: Fortune’s Weave had big shoes to fill, especially considering it’s technically a prequel. Boy does it get SO incredibly close to filling those shoes.

The game’s start honestly isn’t great. It’s not bad by any stretch of the imagination, but the prologue section as a whole was a bit of a weak one, doing the bare minimum amount of scene setting before the game throws you into Part 1 – it’s main focus. While it completely makes sense that the game didn’t really tell you much when it starts to let you find that out yourself some way or another, I do feel like it could have still opened up a little stronger. Then again, most Fire Emblem games do take a little time to warm up to and heat up – I remember Three Houses being a bit of a slow burn (and maybe Awakening too? Can’t remember that one so well… replay incoming?). Once Part 1 gets started, however, the ball does *not* stop rolling. From the moment it starts to the moment it ends, it’s delight after delight and thrilling throughout.
The four main protagonists and their accompanying routes all fell incredibly unique as well, which was a worry I did have before starting the game. Three Houses (I’m mentioning it again, get used to it) had it’s titular three-route structure, and while every character in that game was unique and there were some *pretty big* divergences in some routes, a lot of the work to get to the diverging paths in that game did feel quite repetitive, and made experiencing every path a little less fun than it could have been. Fortune’s Weave does NOT have this problem. Each of the four characters are in Dagsion for the Heroic Games, and they want to win. That’s almost about as similar as they get. There are only a couple of levels in the game that get repeated across routes (when characters meet), and the rest are completely unique to that one character’s story. All of them are there for a completely different reason, and it really works. They have different supporting casts, different free time actions (Cai being able to farm in the open world, Theodora recruiting battalions for battle etc…) and completely different unit types and ‘Blaze Arts’. Their stories don’t even fully revolve around the heroic games as well, having different narrative moments happening and… everything is just DIFFERENT in the best way possible. It’s not like playing the same Part 1 as a different character, it is four completely different stories set at the same time, and each have their own merits.
I, for one, expected to enjoy Cai’s the least and Leda’s the most. The reality was, in fact, the opposite. That’s not to say I didn’t enjoy any of the routes, I loved them ALL, but even the parts I expected not to be as interested in I ended up falling in love with. And that’s completely to do with the incredible voice work they put in. These are award-worthy performances throughout, from almost every single cast member in the entire game. They were SHOWING OFF. Unfortunately, nothing can be perfect. Fortune’s Weave’s story isn’t just Part 1, after all. I won’t mention anything about the plots of further Parts as they aren’t even explicitly detailed in promotional material, and I do think it was better that way. But with the amount of story and content they aimed to pull off in this game, it felt to me like they were trying to tell TOO much in such a span of time. The story and overarching plot were so intricate and detailed that there was no way they could pour over every detail and elaborate on every plot line and character. The game initially, during development, intended to have all *eight* of the main characters (so called Flame Lords) playable, but cut it down to four for the main game. This is painfully obvious in a lot of moments, with specific parts of the plot being left to you to imagine, skipping over major character arcs and narrative developments, major character deaths off screen, and not even exploring the primary antagonists of the game enough. Add in the completely miss-able cutscenes that the game doesn’t tell you about and the poorly patched together lore and story between each part, and the game’s narrative noticeably weakens. It’s not enough to ruin the experience per se, but it gives the otherwise phenomenal story enough plot holes and exposition gaps to leave a slightly bitter taste at times, and a wanting for just a *tiny* bit more.

The game could have also done with a more mature age rating. Being stuck at a PEGI 12 was surprisingly to its detriment. Quite similarly to Three Houses, a lot of the subject matter and themes of Fortune’s Weave are very dark and grim. The game does its absolute best to push the limits of its age rating and express these themes with all they’ve got, and they do achieve their intended tone and atmosphere, but I can imagine it would have been much more impactful if they were allowed to do more than just cutting to black and telling the voice actor: “make a really painfully voiced, bloody death please”. It would have also been nice to have a little more on the side of CG cutscenes, but with a game this big I can understand that their time and budget was probably stretched thin as is. 

One part that the game does pull out all the stops for though is, suitably, the gameplay. It has by far the most complex, intricate, detailed and engaging gameplay of any Fire Emblem released to date. The battles all feel varied and give you enough to think about and strategise without being too overwhelming. It introduces much needed gameplay variety and interesting abilities that completely change the structure and thought process to battles. There are more area effects and terrain difficulties to overcome than ever before, and the scope of battles themselves have also greatly increased. Some of the later battles in the game were frankly huge and such treats to play. Never before have I spent well over an hour on a single Fire Emblem map, let alone enjoyed it as much as I did. Sure, the constant reinforcements in some levels were a little frustrating, but never enough to cause me to get angry with the game itself. In the later part of the game, there is genuinely such a huge sense of strategy and scale that just had me grinning from ear to ear. And don’t get me started on the music. Seriously. I would go on an on about it. Highly memorable, emotion inducing and badass, there is not a single song in the game I didn’t like. While it would’ve been nice to have some more music specifically for cutscenes rather than just repeated tracks that fade out at the end that don’t *quite* have the right atmosphere, what we did get has been put on repeat in my background music playlist I can tell you that. Often I stop working and turn up the volume just because it’s so good. And when the Three Houses themes step in? I. AM. LOCKED. IN.

And another segway into a similarity between Fortune’s Weave and Three Houses – the Free Time mechanic. It is vastly improved in Fortune’s Weave for the most part. It adds a whole lot of good, a little bit of ‘okay’, and does admittedly get rid of a tiny bit of good from Three Houses itself as well. I found myself actually utilising the Free Time so much more in this game, with more things to do, more engaging and less mind-numbing activities, and things that genuinely felt like I was progressing my characters and experience. I did engage in some aspects a lot more than others of course, but for the most time I felt my time was used wisely. While recruitments are a lot more difficult in this game, I also found it a lot easier to tell who I *can* recruit and *how* to do so. I ended up being able to have a lot more people in my arsenal by the end than I would have otherwise. That being said, the free time mechanic, while having differences between each route, did get just as boring as the one in Three Houses eventually. In fact, after just one of the four Part 1 routes, I found myself skipping through free time quite a lot using Auto Activities, kind of feeling a ‘been there done that’.

I won’t say much more about the game, as I feel anything past Part 1 is a case of having to experience it yourself, but pretty much everything mentioned applies to the rest of the game as well, I just tried to phrase it in a… no post-part-1-sense. Fortune’s Weave was a joy to play from (nearly) start to finish, and I was very sad when it ended, despite the minor gameplay frustrations and clear sign of cut content. The post-game depression hit hard for this one, and that ALWAYS means it’s left its mark, and that means it was effective in what it does, and a good game.

*Update 15th October: They just announced the Expansion Pass that will expand the story of the other four Flame Lords. Kind of made me think maybe I should’ve just waited to put this on my website until I’ve played that DLC, but it’s coming out in 2027. I think me myself and I knows that I wouldn’t wait for that. I am unbelievably excited for this expansion, and I hope it fixes all the problems I had with the game’s narrative.*
